Hue scale calibration

The metric treats a hue difference as worth the same everywhere. The chroma round says otherwise - every "too close" verdict in it came from a blue or a magenta, and no warm pair was ever called too close - but there the hue, the chroma and the lightness of a probe all moved together, so none of the three could be credited. This round separates them: hue pairs only, every sector crossed with two chroma levels, and lightness drawn over the whole span the gamut allows at that hue and level, so it varies inside each cell instead of alongside it. Mark every pair you would mix up after living with the palette for a while. Run the log through node data/fit_hue.js log.json. Its own log, separate from the other pages.

Setup

Each round places a hue pair at every distance, in every 60-degree sector, at both chroma levels. Lightness is drawn across the whole range the gamut allows for that hue and level, which is what lets a hue effect be told from a lightness one - the last round could not, because its high chroma reached only the dark blues.
Forty-eight conditions a round - six sectors, two chroma levels, four distances - over twelve palettes, so three rounds is 36. Two rounds already settle the hue term; the third is for the lightness exponent, which this round has to re-measure clean of hue and which two rounds leave marginal. Rounds judged later at these settings pool with earlier ones, so stopping at two and adding the third later costs nothing. Four pairs per palette is safe here where three were needed before: the conditions that miss are spread evenly rather than piling into one cell. The ladder is wider than before because the two ends are far apart - warm pairs were marked three times in twenty around distance 8, cool pairs eighteen times in twenty-three - and one ladder has to straddle both.

Palette

Click two swatches, then mark the pair. Keys: a letter selects, T marks too close, M marginal, Escape clears the selection, Enter goes to the next palette. "One by one" shows each color alone, in random order. The ground is light throughout and does not flip: it is the harder one at both ends of the range, so every mark is the pessimistic verdict and belongs to a ground known in advance. A pair you cannot see is not the same as a pair you cannot tell apart, and only the second is what this measures.
